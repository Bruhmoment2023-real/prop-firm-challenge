# 01 - Prior work and novelty verdict

Checked 13 Oct 2026. Access level is stated for every item: **FULL** (I read the whole text), **ABSTRACT** (abstract only), **META** (title/author/DOI metadata only).

## Verdict

**The scoping pass was wrong: this topic is no longer empty. Sean would not be first.**

- Between roughly April and October 2026 at least eleven working papers on retail prop-firm challenges were posted, ten on SSRN and one of them also on arXiv. They already contain the headline claim (a zero-edge trader passes about 20-45% of the time), the optional-stopping formula, a closed form for trailing drawdowns, cross-firm rule tables and expected-value estimates.
- None of the eleven is peer-reviewed as far as the metadata shows. All are single-author preprints. One peer-reviewed paper exists, but it is sociology with no modelling (Ketmanee 2026).
- So "no peer-reviewed quantitative study" still appears true. "No study" is false and must not be written.

**What is left for Sean to contribute** (ranked by how defensible it is):

1. **The pass as a diagnostic test.** Nobody in the abstracts treats "passed" the way medicine treats a screening test. Sean's pack has: the likelihood ratio of a pass and its bound (it cannot exceed 1 / P(pass | unskilled), which is 3.0 for the common two-step design when the unskilled trader has exactly zero edge; measured values are 1.6-2.4); the posterior probability that a passer is skilled for stated base rates (8-11% when 5% of buyers are skilled); the finding that the test's power comes from the unskilled trader's costs and from small trade sizes, which the firm does not control; and a comparison with a fixed-sample test on the same number of trades, with the observation that a two-barrier rule is a Wald sequential test. Hall (2026) has a "skill gradient" table but no likelihood ratio, no base rates and no test-theory benchmark. **Unverified** for the other preprints because only their abstracts could be read; Tomàs Fernández's abstract ("the selection the evaluation performs") suggests partial overlap.
2. **Independent replication across the CFD/forex firms.** Hall, Villahermosa, Youngblood and Tran model US futures firms. Lim models one stylised two-step contract and then 31 contracts; Tomàs Fernández 13 products. An independent, open, validated re-computation for ten current rule sets from eight firms, read on one date, that reconciles the different published zero-edge numbers (0.30, 0.347, 0.40, 0.36-0.47) is a legitimate replication paper. Replication is valued and is honest about priority.
3. **Sizing invariance stated cleanly.** For static rules with no deadline the zero-edge pass probability does not depend on volatility or bet size at all; it is fixed by the rulebook (1/3 for a 10%/5% target, 10% loss two-step). Volatility only matters through deadlines, discrete monitoring, daily limits and trailing floors. This corrects the original wording of Sean's claim ("pass probability depends on volatility and sizing") and is partly in Villahermosa's abstract already.
4. **A dated record that the rules moved.** FTMO's objectives page shows one rule set for orders "on or after 12 Oct 2026" and a "Legacy" one, the day before access. Time limits, which Lim (2026) found to be the main zero-edge penalty, are stated as absent at seven of the eight firms read (the E8 page did not mention one either way). Small, but real and dated.

**What Sean must not claim:** first study; a new formula; that firms are fraudulent; that the 16.8% or 7.35% published pass rates are comparable to the simulated ones without the denominator caveat.
